## Add RuLawRetrieval (Russian legal article retrieval)

Retrieval of articles of Russian codes of law for questions asked in three styles: everyday
situations without legal terms, short search queries, and lawyer questions.

### Why this fills a gap

The legal domain is already well covered in `mteb` — in every major language except Russian:

```python
import mteb
legal = [t.metadata for t in mteb.get_tasks()
         if t.metadata.type == "Retrieval" and (t.metadata.domains or []) and "Legal" in t.metadata.domains]
print(len(legal))                                                     # 41
print([m.name for m in legal if "rus" in str(m.eval_langs)])          # []
```

41 legal retrieval tasks span `eng`, `fra`, `deu`, `kor`, `zho`, `nld`, `vie`, `ell`, `fin`, `por` —
and none of them is Russian. Filtering the other way round: `mteb` has 21 Russian retrieval tasks
(RuBQ, RiaNews, RuSciBench, MIRACL, mMARCO, MKQA, NeuCLIR, …), and their domains are news,
encyclopaedic, academic and web — none legal. Extending the filter to every modality still returns
no Russian legal task.

This task follows a shape `mteb` already has in other languages — retrieve the statute that answers
a question: `AILAStatutes` (Indian), `GerDaLIR` (German), `LawIRKo` (Korean),
`ZacLegalTextRetrieval` / `TVPLRetrieval` (Vietnamese), `SyntecRetrieval` (French). It covers the
codes people actually search: Labour, Civil, Housing, Administrative Offences, Family, and the
Consumer Rights Protection Law.

### Data

- **Corpus:** 3,786 articles of 6 codes of law from legalacts.ru, edition of September 2026.
  Official texts of laws are not copyrighted (Civil Code of RF, art. 1259 p. 6).
- **Test:** 728 queries, balanced across the three styles and across three slices (articles seen in
  the train split, held-out articles of the same codes, two codes absent from training). The
  dataset also ships a harder split `tk_hard` (716 everyday/search queries on the Labour Code only)
  and a `train` split; this task evaluates `test` only.
- **Queries:** generated by `YandexGPT-5-Lite-8B-instruct` from the article text, then checked by an
  LLM judge from a different family (`Qwen3-8B`), which rejected 12 of 1,039. Filters remove
  queries that name the article number or copy 6+ consecutive words from it.
- **Relevance:** one relevant article per test query. A 174-query subset (split `golden`) was
  re-annotated with multiple relevance; the original automatic labels are kept next to it.
- **Dataset card** with licenses, filters, slices and limitations:
  https://huggingface.co/datasets/alekseevpavel04/ru-law-retrieval
- Code that builds the dataset and reproduces every number:
  https://github.com/alekseevpavel04/ru-law-retrieval

### On synthetic queries

The queries are LM-generated, which `mteb` already accepts for retrieval — 7 text retrieval tasks
carry exactly the `sample_creation="LM-generated and verified"` value used here, including
`WikipediaRetrievalMultilingual`, `SynPerQARetrieval` and the `SynPerChatbot*` family. Three things
were done to keep it honest:

1. the generator for `test` (YandexGPT) and the judge (Qwen3) are from **different model families**,
   and the `train` split is generated by the judge's family — so a model fine-tuned on `train`
   cannot win by matching the test generator's style;
2. lexical overlap between query and gold article is measured and published (0.91 for search
   queries, 0.75 for lawyer questions, 0.28 for everyday ones), so the task is not reducible to
   word matching — BM25 reaches only 0.673;
3. the `golden` re-annotation quantifies the label noise: **about 21% of queries have a second
   relevant article** that the automatic single-article labelling missed. This is a property of
   synthetic retrieval labels generally, and it is stated in the card rather than hidden.

### Known limitations

Stated in the dataset card, repeated here so they are visible in review:

- the queries are synthetic (see the section above for what was done about it);
- the `golden` re-annotation was done by an AI agent working from the article texts, not by a lawyer;
- one relevant article per test query, with ~21% of queries having a second relevant one;
- a single edition of the laws (September 2026); repealed articles are excluded;
- `license="multiple"`: the law texts are public domain under Civil Code art. 1259 p. 6, the
  `test`/`dev` queries are outputs of YandexGPT-5-Lite-8B and carry its licence (which permits
  publishing outputs, with an attribution notice that is reproduced in the card), the `train`
  queries come from Qwen3-8B (Apache-2.0), and the dataset-building code is MIT. Happy to change
  the field if a different value fits `mteb` better.
